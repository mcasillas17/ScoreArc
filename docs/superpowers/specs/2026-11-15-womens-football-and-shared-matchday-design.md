# Women's fútbol and the shared matchday

Design for owner review · November 15, 2026 · E23

## Product decision

One ScoreArc home covers women’s and men’s fútbol. A fan arriving for the first time sees **All**. Choosing **Women** or **Men** narrows the matchday, competition dial and directory. The Women view has an address a fan can bookmark or send to someone else. The owner selected this approach. The URL spelling, control placement, rollout batches and engineering details below are proposals for design approval.

The catalog target is every one of the **45 women’s feeds** in the [coverage audit](../../WOMENS_FOOTBALL_COVERAGE.md#espn-competition-inventory), including college, youth, qualifiers and historical tournaments. The senior Women’s World Cup is one competition with six verified editions and 264 completed matches. The end result includes the teams and players ESPN can identify and the matches they actually played. Coverage can vary by edition and by feature.

**Open for owner review.** For the other 44 feeds, decide whether historical completion includes every verifiable edition or starts at the current edition, with one verified edition for archive-only cups. For example, ESPN lists 14 NWSL seasons. Both options include current coverage and the six senior Women’s World Cups. The choice determines the remaining archive inventory, import budget and completion criteria.

### Approaches considered

| Approach | A fan who follows both América teams | Tradeoff |
|---|---|---|
| **Shared matchday and shareable category views — selected** | Sees both matches in the same feed with their competition names. A friend opening the Women link sees the filtered view immediately. | Broad discovery and a single home. Aggregate reads, navigation and same-name teams need explicit category context. |
| Two dedicated homes | Enters the women’s or men’s home to see that team’s matchday and archive. | Each calendar can lead its own home. Following both teams requires switching homes, or building a third combined experience. |

## What the fan sees

On the existing matchday, place three text links together above the first score card. Use the current type, spacing and focus treatment. Give the selected link an underline and an accessible current-state label. All categories use the same control and the same ScoreArc gold. Crests and league colors retain their meaning. Proposed labels are **All · Women · Men** in English and **Todo · Femenil · Varonil** in Mexican Spanish. The names of competitions supply context on each card. Where names coincide, both sides get explicit labels, such as “Men’s World Cup” and “Women’s World Cup”. Use the same disambiguation in team search results, follows and shares. Avoid repetitive badges beside names that already identify the competition.

```text
All    Women    Men              All    Women    Men
━━━                                     ━━━━━
Today's matches                  Today's matches
Liga MX · América …              Liga MX Femenil · América …
Liga MX Femenil · América …      NWSL · Gotham FC …
```

The sketch illustrates navigation and names. Apply the category **before** the current digest chooses its headline, prioritizes live/upcoming/recent matches, and takes its six cards. Use kickoff order within the upcoming group. Rank equally across categories and deduplicate before counting the headline. A quiet women’s matchday can say that no matches are scheduled in the window and link to the women’s competition directory, including the World Cup archive. If every eligible match feed failed, show the unavailable state. Keep available rows and identify partial coverage when only some feeds failed.

| Surface | Behavior |
|---|---|
| Home | The headline, matches, leaderboards, relevant news and visible followed teams reflect the selected category. Filtering follows changes only what the fan sees. “All” restores both sets of followed teams. An archive contributes zero live-feed requests. |
| Competition dial | Retain the desktop rail and the phone wheel. Narrow competition items before computing focus, rest position and keyboard targets. The Teams dial keeps its letter navigation over the visible teams. A visible text link **Browse competitions** opens a lightweight index grouped by domestic leagues, club cups, national teams, and youth/college. It uses registry metadata and lists every published competition in the selected category, including archives. A fan can reach any published competition without turning through 57 crests. |
| Teams and players | Scope team search, chips and counts before loading memberships. Search “América” in All and the two teams have distinct accessible names, links and follow actions. A player directory stays scoped to one competition and season, with its roster coverage count. Matchday appearances can link a player represented on both a national side and a club through the same canonical identity. |
| News | Scope source reads and article attribution. Publish a new competition’s news when its articles can be attributed to it. The Femenil sample returned general fútbol news. Exclude those stories from both the competition page and Women digest. |
| Competition, team, player and archive pages | Keep existing competition-scoped routes. Use the correct EN/ES terms for the selected competition, including player directories and champion cards. Edition links, local kickoff times, photo fallbacks, share text, OG identity and direct-entry navigation must agree. A completed World Cup edition stays reachable from the competition index year-round. |

**URL and history.** Use `/en` and `/es` for All, `/en?view=women` and `/es?view=women` for Women and the equivalent `view=men` links. Teams, News and the proposed `/[locale]/competitions` index accept the same parameter. These are server-rendered links. A cold open, reload, locale switch, and browser Back/Forward reconstruct the selection from the URL. Scope changes create history entries. Preserve valid unrelated search parameters. An absent, unknown or repeated category resolves to All. Keep the home, Teams, News and competition-index metadata, canonical URLs and language alternates consistent with each valid scope.

Links into a competition or entity keep its canonical category-free URL. Its registry entry identifies the category on direct entry. From a detail page, the dial and outward collection links default to that category. Browser Back returns to the originating collection and its actual filter. Selecting All from a detail page opens the All matchday. A saved filter or an account is unnecessary for sharing.

## Catalog, identity and services

- **One registry.** Add an explicit women’s/men’s category to competition records. Assign all 12 existing competitions explicitly. Model club/national and age or college level independently. Extend the TypeScript-to-Go export and its contract tests. Each edition carries its actual format and publishable features. Keep a per-edition distinction between candidate, staging ingestion, published live coverage and published archive. Candidate entries generate no public links, public reader resources, automatic polls or alerts. Staging gets an explicit allowlist in the staging environment. An archive is publicly readable and excluded from live polling, alerts and the home digest. Existing scopes start with equivalent public behavior; reconcile any change to an already monitored scope separately. A temporary provider failure changes observed availability, never the editorial publication state.
- **Identity and storage.** Keep the existing Postgres tables and source crosswalks for the pilot. The audit tested those writes and reads with the existing roles. Curate distinct IDs for the two América teams, Mexico’s men’s, women’s and youth sides, and similarly named clubs. Reuse a women’s club ID and player UUID across league and cup. Update the seed generator and seed-drift checks, then regenerate both the Go competition registry and frontend team crosswalk. Preserve existing URLs and stored follows. Unresolved provider identities stay unlinked pending curation. Player names and crests are inadequate identity keys.
- **Ingestion and reader.** Keep the existing Go worker, reader, Neon and R2. Pass per-edition capabilities to both binaries. Source cup entrants from validated edition membership or deduplicated match participants when standings are unavailable. The same membership must reach the team/player index. Keep historical squads grounded in matchday appearances and show current rosters only in their stated scope. Preserve the reader's distinctions among empty, partial, stale and unavailable, including through ESPN fallback. Backfill historical matches with explicit competition/edition/date bounds, a restartable checkpoint, and idempotent writes through the store and its finalization invariants. The existing play backfill acts on matches already stored. It cannot populate the World Cup archive on its own.
- **World Cup and playoffs.** Start with the 2023 Women’s World Cup arc, then expose each verified edition with its own groups and bracket size. Verify the historical `quarter-finals`, `semi-finals` and `3rd-place` aliases in both mappers. Count the two postponed 2007 records separately from its 32 completed matches. Serve season-scoped standings and leaders only with matching edition evidence. Keep the 2027 qualifier/playoff feeds separate from the senior finals until ESPN publishes those matches. For NWSL distinguish Shield and single-elimination playoffs. For Apertura 2026 Femenil take four from each of two groups, handle two legs and aggregate advancement. Verify its actual phase labels through finalization before publishing a champion.
- **Operations.** Filter eligible editions and supported resources before scheduling work. Limit simultaneous upstream reads and retain cache reuse on Home, Teams and News. Use the existing single-source operation/fallback rules. Keep R2’s public crest handling and private play archive separate. Require observed per-match geometry before any shot visual. Measure cold-cycle time, simultaneous finals, existing competitions’ freshness, DB pool pressure, archived bytes and reader request budgets at each increase. The alert sender budgets 16 sends per run and the watchdog accepts 32 scopes. Handle delivery backlog and checkpoint changes before the corresponding live cohort crosses those bounds. A 57-entry catalog does not imply 57 live scopes.

## Delivery and acceptance

The following batches cover the entire inventory. Promotions may be split further when editions have different coverage. The owner’s existing E18/E23 source-rights and certification decisions apply to publication. Engineering can work on the registry, UI, synthetic contract cases and edition checks independently. A public score-only competition remains a valid outcome when that is the evidence available. Missing rosters or player histories remain explicit coverage gaps.

| Batch | Competitions | Acceptance example |
|---|---|---|
| Shared foundations | Existing 12, with women’s candidate records | Category links work with server rendering and keyboard navigation. Existing bookmarks and follows resolve. Adding an unpublished candidate triggers zero public-data requests and creates no alert scope. |
| First four | NWSL, Liga MX Femenil, English WSL, senior Women’s World Cup | Complete current team crosswalks for the three leagues. Trace a match → team → player in EN/ES with both stores. Reconcile all 64 completed matches in the 2023 World Cup, including its 16 knockout/placement matches. Check the NWSL and Femenil arcs against their distinct rules. |
| Remaining domestic leagues | Six other domestic feeds in the audit | Check every standings club’s roster availability for each edition. Publish truthful counts, rules and stage names. |
| Club cups | All 15 domestic/continental cup and playoff feeds in the audit, starting with UWCL and Concacaf W Champions Cup | Reuse a club across league and cup. A team can be discovered and a roster reached in a knockout-only competition with a verified team list. Check two-leg ties and qualification rounds independently. |
| National competitions and archive | Remaining 18 senior national feeds, including qualifiers, Olympics, regional championships and friendlies. Complete the five older senior World Cup editions under the same competition ID. | Reconcile 264 completed matches across the six editions. A historical match never presents today’s roster as its historical lineup. Edition-specific qualifiers get their own scope. |
| Youth and college | FIFA U-17 Women’s World Cup and NCAA women’s soccer | Confirm distinct youth team identities. NCAA exceeded both the 1,000-event monthly fetch cap and the reader’s 2,000-match season bound. Use bounded date partitions and paged reads with no dropped or duplicated IDs. No all-school roster fan-out on Home or Teams. |

For each published edition, verify the usable competition name and mark, edition-aware matches, team identities, permitted feature list, public routes, and resource metadata. For live coverage, observe a real live-to-finished transition and a healthy existing scope in the same busy window. For archived coverage, compare source and stored event IDs, status counts and knockout winners. If attribution or source rights are insufficient, retain the evidence and the candidate entry until the missing condition is resolved. **Interface acceptance.** Check the shared and filtered views in EN/ES, at low/middle/high phone widths and on desktop, including keyboard-only use and screen-reader link names. Verify the dial after category changes, a zero-result team search, an empty matchday, a feed outage, both América results and follows, and a cold-open shared Women link. Check that a filtered page makes zero data calls for excluded competitions. Recheck share cards and bump the existing OG version if their rendering changes.

**Rollback.** Withdraw the affected public promotion, pause its live polling and reconcile its monitor scope and pending alerts. Keep entity IDs and retained history. A transient data outage uses the existing resource status rather than removing a published URL. Rehearse removal of a monitored scope separately from adding one.

**Builder decisions.** File boundaries, CSS details, concurrency below the measured budgets, and the historical import checkpoint implementation belong in the implementation plan. The existing ESPN default and staged reader-cutover decision apply throughout. This design introduces no new account, third-party API product or gender inference for an individual player.
